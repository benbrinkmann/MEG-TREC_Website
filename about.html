About MEG-TREC

The MEG Translational Research Consortium

Clinicians, researchers, and industry partners working together in magnetoencephalography

Mission

Why the Consortium Exists

MEG-TREC brings together people who use magnetoencephalography (MEG) in clinical care, research, and product development. Its purpose is to share advances in MEG technology and its applications in neurological research and patient care, and to build the collaborations that move new methods into clinical use.

Recent consortium meetings have focused on applications in epilepsy, neurodevelopment, aging, and neurodegenerative disease, along with current advances in MEG technology. Discussions at the 2025 meeting led to new partnerships between academia and industry around data sharing, advanced analytics, and the validation of MEG biomarkers for regulatory approval.

History

From the MEG Texas Consortium

The consortium began as the MEG Texas Consortium. As participation grew beyond Texas, it was renamed the MEG Translational Research Consortium to reflect its national reach and its growing impact in MEG research, clinical applications, and technology development.

The board now includes members from academic medical centers and children's hospitals in Tennessee, Florida, Texas, Michigan, Arizona, and Minnesota. Recent annual meetings have been hosted by the University of Florida in Gainesville (2025) and by Le Bonheur Children's Hospital in Memphis (2026).

Activities

What We Do

Annual Meeting

A yearly scientific meeting with keynote lectures, panel presentations, debates, and time for networking. Registration has been free with an RSVP.

Collaboration

A forum for MEG centers to compare clinical practices, share data and methods, and start multicenter projects.

Education

Educational material for trainees, clinicians, and researchers who are new to MEG, including our MEG principles and techniques page.

Interested in Participating?

The consortium welcomes clinicians, scientists, trainees, and industry professionals with an interest in MEG. Please reach out to a member of the board.
